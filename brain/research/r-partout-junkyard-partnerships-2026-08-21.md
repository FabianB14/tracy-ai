## Findings

- Row52 is the incumbent for searching SELF-SERVICE yard inventory: vehicle search,
  VIN decode, and arrival alerts. [1]
- Row52's coverage has shrunk to roughly 50 Pick-n-Pull branded yards nationwide,
  per hobbyist reports of the change. Independent self-service yards are largely
  unsearchable today. [2]
- Car-Part.com owns the FULL-SERVICE side: parts locating for yards that pull parts
  for repair shops, plus software and web hosting for recyclers. Higher prices,
  pro-oriented. [7][1]
- Yards run on vertical yard management systems: Hollander Powerlink dominates,
  Pinnacle Professional is second, Checkmate serves smaller yards. These handle
  purchasing, dismantling, interchange, and point of sale, with deep Car-Part.com
  integration. [3][4]
- Washington market size: an unofficial directory lists 1,337 junkyard and salvage
  entries in WA. [5] The WA Department of Licensing licenses "motor vehicle
  wreckers" but publishes no public count on its licensing page. [6]

## Contradictions and risks

- The 1,337 WA figure is a directory count, not a licensing count, and likely
  inflates by including towing and scrap operations. Official number still unknown.
- Row52 retreating to Pick-n-Pull only is ambiguous: it may mean indie-yard
  coverage is a hard, low-margin problem, not just an underserved one.
- Full-service yards are locked into Powerlink plus Car-Part. PartOut's realistic
  wedge is self-service yards and small independents that never inventory
  individual parts at all, which is also the segment with least software budget.
- Willingness to pay (rev share or SaaS fee) is completely unvalidated.

## Confidence rationale

The competitive landscape (Row52, Car-Part, the YMS oligopoly) is corroborated
across independent sources. Market size for WA is soft and first-party validation
from yards is absent, so 0.55.
